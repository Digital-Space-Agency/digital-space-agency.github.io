WBS AI Agents & Automations

n8n workflows and MCP-based AI agents for back-office and engineering work: schema-validated tool calls, retries and monitoring.

Most small teams lose hours to work that is predictable but not simple: moving data between tools, checking it, and chasing exceptions. I build agents and workflows that do it, with the same release discipline as a shipped mobile app.

What it is

I build n8n workflows and MCP-based AI agents for back-office and engineering work, and I am completing WBS Coding School's AI Agents programme. The same agents and workflows run Digital Space Agency itself.

My role

I design, build and monitor automations for my own studio and for clients.

What I built

  • n8n workflows and Python-based AI agents for back-office and engineering work, including the agents that run Digital Space Agency itself.
  • Completed WBS Coding School's AI Agents and Automation programme (July to October 2026): two applied business-workflow prototypes and a final MVP combining n8n, Python, APIs and AI agents to solve a real business problem end to end.

Architecture and approach

  • n8n for orchestration: triggers, branching, retries and error workflows.
  • Model Context Protocol (MCP) to give agents controlled access to external tools and data.
  • Multi-agent collaboration where a planner hands work to specialised agents.
  • Schema-validated tool calls, retries, idempotent steps and alerts, so a failure is visible and recoverable.
  • Testing, review and production-readiness applied to workflows, the same as to mobile releases.

Hard problems

Making agents dependable

Models are not deterministic. Reliability comes from narrow tools, validated inputs and outputs, retries and a human in the loop for anything irreversible.

Observability

A silent automation is a risk. Every workflow reports success and failure somewhere a person will see it.

Outcome

  • Working MVP delivered as the final project of the programme.
  • Used in my own studio daily.
